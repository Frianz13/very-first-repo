Imagine the scenario. You walk into a department store and within seconds a salesperson approaches you and with unbridled enthusiasm says this, "Hey, welcome to ABC. My name is Josh.

How can I help you?" If you're like most people, you're going to retreat because that energy feels off-putting. It's like you're going to be told what to do or pressured into buying something. As sellers, we create this by what we say and what our intent is.

On a cold call, hey, my name is Josh. the reason for my call is creates the same reaction. So what's the way out of this?

It starts by changing our intent. When our intent is to sell everyone and assume that everyone needs what we're buying, we sound pushy because intent affects what we say. But if we change our intent from how can I sell to is there a problem here?

It changes everything because we're no longer assuming what we have is needed. We're shifting from convincing and explaining to asking a neutral question about a potential problem without leading people to a desired answer. So, here's what that might sound like on a cold call.

Prospect picks up. Let's say it's me. In a calm voice, the seller says this.

Hey, Josh. My name is Nick. we've never spoken before, but I was on your LinkedIn, been following you for a while, and was wondering if I could ask you a question.

I would say, "Sure, how could I help you, Nick?" And then Nick says this, "So Josh, over the course of 3 months, your posts have had a little over 4,100 comments. How are you identifying the commenters that might be a fit for your workshop so you can reach out to them proactively? Is that something you're doing manually, or do you have a way to surface them and shut the front door and listen.

You're not assuming, you're exploring. You're asking a question that makes people scratch their head and think, "hm, I'm not sure. That's a good question.

What do you mean?" And now we're in a conversation. You will start way more conversations when you come from a place of, "Is there a problem?" and assuming there is one. Buyers have the answers.
