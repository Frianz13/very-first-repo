## Post 1 | ~2026-06-01 (≈2 weeks before collection)
- URL : https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundsales_sales-activity-7465024444596486145-15qR
- Tactic : Cold email openers using "diagnostic questions."Generic questions ("Are you struggling to keep up with the competition?") get ignored; specific ones tied to a real, measurable gap ("Can you tell from your CRM which accounts are close to churning before they tell you?") make the reader actually pause. His point: don't lean on AI gimmicks or personalization tricks, understand the buyer's real process gaps first.
- Why I saved it : It shows good cold email starts from really knowing the buyer, not from tricks which is how I think about reaching out to strangers.

## Post 2 | ~2026-05-25 (≈3 weeks before collection)
- URL : https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundsales_sales-activity-7462125341667409920-yE-0
- Tactic : A repeatable cold email framework with 3 parts: (1) a diagnostic question that makes the reader self-reflect, (2) a specific, tangible outcome they actually want, (3) a "disarming" CTA that breaks the usual sales-email pattern. He stresses the whole thing lives or dies on getting the diagnostic question right (takes lots of iteration).
- Why I saved it : A clear, reusable structure, the kind of repeatable system that fits how I like to work.

## Post 3 | ~2026-05-15 (≈1 month before collection)
- URL : https://www.linkedin.com/posts/outboundsales_cold-call-script-generator-activity-7452340618049204224-Zc02
- Tactic : "Warm" cold calling, instead of dialing blind, his team calls people who already replied to an email or LinkedIn message. Replies auto-route to a Slack channel so reps can call within minutes while the prospect is still warm, using the reply as context. (Also links a free cold-call script generator at sellbetter.xyz.)
- Why I saved it : It links email and calling into one pipeline instead of treating them separately, close to my own "warm first" instinct.
